# Good Books I Read in 2018

#### Five stars: highly recommended (in order of recommendation)

- [Why We Get Sick](https://book.douban.com/subject/30164677/ "Why We Get Sick") by Randolph M. Nesse and George C. Williams

Category: **Broad reading | Life sciences | Popular science**

![Why We Get Sick: Chinese edition cover](assets/images/reading-2018/a467af1e6e29a184b0e7111264e59279.jpg)

Review: Excellent popular science, looking at medicine and biology from an evolutionary/Darwinian perspective and proposing meaningful conjectures and questions. The evolutionary bugs in the human eye, respiratory tract and esophagus are classics; not long ago I saw someone on Zhihu use them to argue against intelligent design. More than twenty years have passed since the first English edition in 1994, and I am quite interested in what related developments evolutionary biology has seen since. It is even more engaging alongside knowledge of genetic programming and genetic engineering—two entirely different fields despite both being “genetic”—or when you happen to be ill yourself. Just kidding.

Appendix: [excerpts from the whole book](https://book.douban.com/annotation/66339095/).

#### Four stars: good (most recent reading first)

- [Programming Collective Intelligence: Building Smart Web 2.0 Applications](https://book.douban.com/subject/2209702/ "Programming Collective Intelligence") **Intensive study | Computing | Data mining**

  Review: Covers some common data mining algorithms. The title seems somewhat misleading next to the content, unless every dataset counts as “collective.” It skims over the various algorithms. This time I used it as an index from which to search and consolidate some basic knowledge. Some application scenarios and details are well explained, but without the corresponding technical terms or references, beginners struggle to go deeper. I didn't really get into the code and experiments, except for the implementation in the genetic programming chapter. I gained a lot from the random optimization chapter: cost functions, representations of constrained problems, and similar solutions yielding similar results.

- [Kokoro](https://book.douban.com/subject/27135380/ "Kokoro") **Fiction | Novel | Japan**

  Review: One review put it well: indecision and ambiguity come from distrust of oneself and others, eventually leading people to hand their fate over to the external world.

- [The Inevitable](https://book.douban.com/subject/26658379/ "The Inevitable") **Broad reading | Technology**

  Review: Very good ideas, some exactly the same as points in the articles I am writing… One star off for American-style verbosity, but it did provide some material for my writing.

  Appendix: [excerpts](https://book.douban.com/people/simoncos/annotation/26658379/).

- [Dune](https://book.douban.com/subject/26836970/ "Dune") **Fiction | Novel | Science fiction**

  Review: Interesting worldbuilding, with a strong legendary quality.

- [尽头 (The End)](https://book.douban.com/subject/25762598/ "尽头") **Broad reading | Literature | Second reading**

  Review: Second reading. Tang Nuo now does seem a little verbose and showy with his erudition. But what he wants to express is complex; perhaps redundancy and a network of connections are the only way to approach precision? One star off, but still worth reading.

  Appendix: [excerpts](https://book.douban.com/people/simoncos/annotation/25762598/).

- [Improv Wisdom: Don't Prepare, Just Show Up](https://book.douban.com/subject/2887103/ "Improv Wisdom") **Broad reading | Methodology**

  Review: Aligns with some of my thoughts and practices. Improvisation is relaxed concentration. In the author's account, it includes both flow itself and ways to increase the chance of entering flow. Even blind optimism is better than never starting. Putting doing before thinking is something I hope to manage. The view that humor has little value in itself is also worth thinking about.

  Appendix: [excerpts](https://book.douban.com/people/simoncos/annotation/2887103/).

- [The Deer and the Cauldron (five volumes)](https://book.douban.com/subject/1559310/ "The Deer and the Cauldron") **Fiction | Novel | Wuxia**

  Review: Jin Yong's plotting is certainly solid, although sometimes rather rambling. The seven wives feel assigned by decree; in the last dozen or so chapters they become complete extras. Like the Sun Moon Holy Cult, the Mystic Dragon Cult has traces of its real-world model that perceptive readers should recognize. The male chauvinism remains severe. The best emotional writing may actually be between Wei Xiaobao and Kangxi.

- [Think Python: How to Think Like a Computer Scientist](https://book.douban.com/subject/26634683/ "Think Python") **Intensive study | Computing | Python**

  Review: After three years writing Python, this is still the first book about it I have finished, and I learned from it. At my current level of understanding, the book reads smoothly and gets to the essentials. Everything it introduces is explained thoroughly, with clear definitions, continuity between concepts, and the author's practical experience and deep understanding. Each chapter ends with exercises and debugging advice. Even someone who has never encountered a programming language could start writing code with this book.

- [Linked (tenth-anniversary edition)](https://book.douban.com/subject/24862722/ "Linked") **Broad reading | Popular science | Complex systems**

  Review: A very good introduction to early complex network research and its applications. Without background knowledge, you can enjoy the spectacle; with it, the book serves as a simple survey. One gain is seeing some of the early history of complex networks, particularly the move from static random graphs to growth mechanisms—nodes appearing and disappearing—plus preferential linking. It also helped me appreciate the widespread presence of complex networks and the discovery of power laws in all kinds of networks. Any complex system may well have a network behind it. The spread and treatment of AIDS and systems of gene–protein interactions are quite interesting. The Achilles' heel section is also fascinating: scale-free networks governed by power laws withstand random attacks or failures well, but targeted attacks on hubs overwhelm them. Which end of the dice lands on an unimportant or important node becomes critical. But what if a hub's interior is itself a fairly decentralized subnetwork, as behind Google.com? Although some passages become cluttered with too many citations, overall this is a very good book for popular science or an introduction to research.

  Appendix: [excerpts](https://book.douban.com/people/simoncos/annotation/24862722/).

- [笑傲江湖 (The Smiling, Proud Wanderer, four volumes)](https://book.douban.com/subject/1002299/ "笑傲江湖") **Fiction | Novel | Wuxia**

  Review: A little scattered, with too much unnecessary peripheral material and abrupt turns in much of the main plot. The good parts include learning swordsmanship, Plum Manor, and portions of the relationships with the three women. Yang Lianting has a big beard, haha.

- [Story: Substance, Structure, Style, and the Principles of Screenwriting](https://book.douban.com/subject/25976544/ "Story") **Broad reading | Creativity | Screenwriting**

  Review: A good book. For an outsider like me, it offered a reasonably systematic understanding of film scripts and stories, helping me appreciate others' work and examine my own ideas.

  Appendix: [excerpts](https://book.douban.com/people/simoncos/annotation/25976544/).

Problem Solving
and Design

In 2016–2017, I wrote a series of short essays on Jianshu. Starting with a psychology textbook’s account of the factors that affect problem solving, I wanted to think through problem solving and design together. Ten years later, four of those essays have become pages you can play with: condensed text, each built around a few small experiments to drag, click, and calculate.

About this series

The originals were notes made while reading and thinking. These adaptations preserve their arguments and voice while reducing the text to roughly 60% of its original length. The interactions have two sources. Figures in the first three essays are redrawn as interactive versions, with the originals shown alongside. Part 4 turns its two illustrations—the expanded rock-paper-scissors game from The Big Bang Theory and Fire Emblem’s weapon triangle—into examples in the preference explorer, without reproducing the images. Experiments and examples previously described only in words, such as the water jars, search tree, and real harbour-crossing routes, have been added here. Sources and estimated figures are identified on each page.

The original essays are also on this site, with English translations. Apart from a few typos corrected in 2026, their text is unchanged: The Problem Situation, Feasible Solutions, Open Problems, Choice and Optima.

Colors mean the same thing throughout: amber marks things you can manipulate, mint green marks answers, and red marks elimination or contradiction.
